The LinkedIn agent skill: your posts, comments and week, written by Claude

Eleven free Claude skills for one LinkedIn account. They write your posts off 21 hook formulas, comment on other people's posts without "Great post!", score your profile out of 100, plan your week and run every draft through a humanizer before you see it. You still hit post yourself.

1Install it

Easiest: paste this into Claude and send it.

https://github.com/Jakeschincariol/linkedin-agent-skill

Install this skill, then confirm /li-post works.

Or yourself, in Claude Code (terminal):

git clone https://github.com/Jakeschincariol/linkedin-agent-skill.git
cp -r linkedin-agent-skill/skills/li-* ~/.claude/skills/

Or as a plugin, inside Claude Code:

/plugin marketplace add Jakeschincariol/linkedin-agent-skill
/plugin install linkedin-agent
No Claude Code? Paste any single SKILL.md from the repo at the top of a normal Claude chat and it runs as a mode. You lose the two Python tools of /li-human (the cleaner and the five-check scorer), but the rest works.

2Spend ten minutes on your voice

Every skill reads one file: ~/.claude/linkedin/voice.md. Copy templates/voice.md from the repo there and fill it in, or paste three of your own posts into Claude and say "write my voice.md from these". Skip it and everything comes out sounding like everyone else.

3Start with these four

/li-postOne idea into a post: three hook options from 21 formulas, one full draft, humanized before you see it.
/li-commentComments on other people's posts. Nine comment types, picked by what the post actually is. Never "Great post!".
/li-profileScores your profile against a 12-part rubric out of 100, then rewrites in fix-first order. Most first-pass profiles land in the 30s and 40s.
/li-planThe week: what to post, when to post it, and the 10 people to engage with. Writes ~/.claude/linkedin/plan.md.

4Run the humanizer on anything

/li-human ships two Python scripts with no dependencies. They run on your machine and nothing is uploaded.

python3 humanize.py draft.txt --report   # clean it, show every change
python3 detect.py draft.txt               # score it, five checks
python3 detect.py before.txt after.txt    # prove the delta

It removes invisible characters, turns em dashes into commas, straightens quotes and swaps 113 stock phrases ("delve", "leverage", "in today's fast-paced world") for plain English. Sentence shapes like "It's not just X, it's Y" get flagged for you to rewrite. The five checks: burstiness, specificity, slop density, fingerprint, voice. In the README demo a terrible draft goes from 24.8 (flagged) to 69.7 after one pass.

The other six

  • /li-reply sorts the comments under your post into lead, substance, peer, support and noise, then writes replies in that order
  • /li-carousel document posts: slide copy, the cover and the PDF to upload
  • /li-repurpose one video, newsletter or transcript into a week of posts
  • /li-dm the 200-character invite note, the first message and two follow-ups
  • /li-inbox triages your inbox into lead, recruiter, peer, ask and spam
  • /li-audit post-mortem on what you already published, ranked by engagement rate and reach multiple

Honest notes

  • It does not post for you. Every skill ends in a copy-ready block and you paste it. Automating LinkedIn through a browser or third-party tool breaks LinkedIn's User Agreement and gets accounts restricted, so the repo deliberately does not do it.
  • The five checks are local heuristics, not GPTZero, Originality or Turnitin. Nobody can honestly promise "undetectable".
  • It never invents numbers under your name. If a draft needs a figure you did not give, it comes back with {{your number}} and a flag.
