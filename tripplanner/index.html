Trip Planner: Claude plans your whole trip, hour by hour, and checks its own work

Planning a trip usually means a pile of open tabs: flights, hotels, opening hours, holidays, trains, currency. This free Claude Code skill does all of it from one sentence and hands you a finished plan: real flight prices across dates, every day timed down to the walk, hotels by neighbourhood, a budget and a booking checklist. Then it checks the plan for mistakes before you see it.

1Install it

The easy way: open Claude Code and paste this:

Install the skill from https://github.com/skywain/trip-planner-skill into ~/.claude/skills/trip-planner

Or do it yourself with one command (the README's way):

git clone https://github.com/skywain/trip-planner-skill.git ~/.claude/skills/trip-planner

Optional, for the flight price scanner and the picture pipeline:

pip3 install --user fast-flights Pillow
Needs: Python 3.9 or newer (the scripts only use the standard library). Without fast-flights you still get a Google Flights link instead of a price grid.

2Plan a trip in one sentence

In Claude Code, just say where and when. It triggers on its own for trip, flight and itinerary requests, or call it directly:

/trip-planner Japan, 8 days in late November from London, mid budget, history and food

If something essential is missing (where, when, from where) it asks once. Then it shows you two or three route options, you pick one, and it builds the rest. Say "just plan it, don't ask" and it skips the questions and lists its assumptions at the top instead.

3Get the finished page

The result is not a wall of text: it's a designed, phone-friendly page in one of eight themes (illustrated, clay, noir, glass, journal, zine, splash, portal), plus a calendar file (.ics) with reminders for every booking deadline and an offline map (.kml) for Organic Maps or Google My Maps. Every price and opening time carries its source and date, or a "verify at link" flag.

What it does for you

FlightsScans a grid of dates for real prices (Google Flights, no key needed) and decides train vs. fly for every leg between cities.
Hours + holidaysChecks opening hours, closure days and public holidays with tools, not memory. In the Japan example it caught that Shinjuku Gyoen is closed on Mondays and that 23 November is a national holiday.
Every day, hour by hourTimes, transfers, walking distance, a rain alternative and what to drop if you run late, with a tap-to-navigate map link on every hop.
Hotels + budgetRecommends neighbourhoods with dated booking links (not invented nightly rates), adds up the whole trip in your home currency and gives you a checklist sorted by deadline.
Self-checkBefore delivery it attacks its own plan: closures, last-entry times, walking totals, map pins. In the Japan example it found and fixed nine issues.

Honest notes

  • The skill is free (MIT) and all its data sources are free and keyless. Claude Code itself needs a Claude plan.
  • It never books or pays for you and never enters personal data. You get links and a checklist, you click.
  • Flight prices are comparison-grade from a cache; the booking link is the source of truth.
  • Pictures: with no image generator it uses the built-in stock kit (free) and says so on the page. With your own OpenRouter key, custom art costs about $0.25 to $0.46 per trip (the README's numbers).
  • Only tested in Claude Code so far; other agents should work but are untested.
  • The chat prompt, the flight prices for the other dates and the list of browser tabs in the Instagram video are illustrations; the Japan trip (days, places, walking kilometres, budget, the nine self-check fixes) comes from the example in the repo.
