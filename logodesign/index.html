logo-design-skill: Claude designs your logo and hands you the brand kit

Tell Claude about your business. It asks the questions a designer would ask, studies how logos in your industry look so yours stands apart, builds three concepts in SVG, tests each one down to 16 pixels and recommends one. Pick your favourite and you get colours, app icons, a favicon, mockups and brand guidelines.

GitHub: kaankiziltug/logo-design-skillMITClaude Code · Claude.ai · Claude Desktop

Repo with 18 full example runs: github.com/kaankiziltug/logo-design-skill

1Install it

Claude Code: paste this and Claude sets it up for you:

Install this skill for me: https://github.com/kaankiziltug/logo-design-skill

Or run the two commands the README recommends (plugin marketplace):

/plugin marketplace add kaankiziltug/logo-design-skill
/plugin install logo-design@logo-design-skill

Claude.ai or Claude Desktop: download logo-design.zip from the Releases page and upload it under Settings → Capabilities → Skills. If the upload is too big, use logo-design-lite.zip (everything except the reference SVG files).

You need Python 3.8+ for the test and export tools (standard library only). PNG and favicon export uses whatever renderer you have: cairosvg, rsvg-convert, Inkscape, a Chromium browser like Chrome or Edge, or macOS Quick Look.

2Ask for your logo

Just describe the business. These are example requests from the README:

Design a logo for Harbor, a savings app for first-time savers. It should feel calm and safe.
Give me three wordmark directions for a specialty coffee roaster called Kiln.
Here's our current logo (logo.svg). Critique it and suggest a refresh.

Give it a name, what you do, three words for the feel and anything to avoid. If you leave things out, it asks or states its assumptions.

3Pick a direction, get the kit

The skill always stops at a checkpoint: one overview image with the concepts at real 64, 32 and 16 px sizes and a recommendation. Nothing else gets made until you choose. Then ask for the kit:

Go with concept B. Make the full kit: colours, lockups, favicon, app icon and a one-page usage guide.

What's inside

1,400+ reference logosAbout 1,200 brands, each classified by mark type, technique, geometry, mood and industry. Used to learn category conventions and avoid look-alikes, never to copy.
Test sheetsEvery concept is shown at 96 and 32 px, in one colour and reversed, then walked down a size ladder to 16 px, plus squint, mirror and a competitor shelf test.
svg_audit.pyTurns craft rules into checks and gives each file a production-readiness score out of 100 (in the README's run: 99/100 for the clean lockup, 76/100 for an emblem with 374 anchor points).
The kitPresentation boards with six industry-specific mockups, black, white and one-colour versions, favicon, app icon and a full web icon set, plus a brand guidelines template.

Honest notes

  • The skill is free (MIT). The reference logos in the library belong to their brands and are included for study only, not covered by the MIT license.
  • It's a strong first draft and a disciplined process, not a trademark check. Before you print a logo on everything, search whether something similar is already registered in your market.
  • The skill is brand new (first release 26 Sep 2026), so expect updates.
  • The concepts, the Kiln kit and the mockups in the Instagram video are illustrations drawn for the video; the numbers (1,400+ logos, 16 px test, the 99 and 76 audit scores) come from the README.
